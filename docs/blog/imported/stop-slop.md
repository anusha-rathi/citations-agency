# Stop Slop — AI writing tells to eliminate

Source document supplied by AR, 29 July 2026. **Every piece of content on this
site must be passed through this before publishing**, including tool reviews,
blog posts, learn pages and category blurbs.

Audit run 29 July 2026 found: "The short version" used as a closing heading in
11 separate posts, 325 em dashes across posts and reviews, 12 of 18 titles
matching banned patterns. See docs/seo-strategy.md section 5.

Comprehensive Analysis of AI-Generated Writing Tells
EDITING INSTRUCTIONS
When using this document to edit AI-generated text:
 1. Go through EACH section systematically
 2. Search for and eliminate every pattern listed
 3. Make multiple passes - patterns often hide behind other patterns
 4. Don't just swap one template for another (e.g., replacing "Moreover" with "Picture this:")
 5. Check that fixes don't introduce new tells
Title Patterns
    Colon addiction: "Coding with AI: A Practical Snapshot" (classic AI title structure)
    X and Y formulations: "Benefits and Challenges of..." "Opportunities and Risks in..."
    The [Adjective] [Noun] construction: "The Digital Transformation" "The Essential Guide"
    Gerund openings: "Understanding...", "Navigating...", "Exploring..."
    How to/Why/What titles: Formulaic question-based titles
Opening Patterns
    Scene-setting openers: "Picture this:", "Imagine:", "Consider this scenario:"
    In medias res attempts: "You're staring at..." (trying too hard to be engaging)
    Temporal markers: "In today's world," "In recent years," "As we move forward"
    Question openers: "Have you ever wondered...?" "What if I told you...?"
Syntactic Patterns
Sentence Structure
    Hypotactic over-complexity: Excessive subordinate clauses connected by conjunctions ("while,"
    "although," "whereas"), creating unnecessarily complex sentences
    Front-loaded transitions: Starting 80% of sentences with discourse markers ("Moreover,"
    "Furthermore," "Additionally," "However")
    Parallel structure obsession: Rigid adherence to matching grammatical forms ("to explore, to
    examine, and to evaluate")
   Em dash dependency: Using em dashes as a crutch for pseudo-profound contrasts ("not X — but
   Y")
       Watch for: "Clear the grunt work – Generate boilerplate..."
       Watch for: "Translate plain English into code – Type..."
       These persist even after "humanizing" edits!
   Contraction patterns: Overusing "we're," "you're," "it's" to sound conversational
       Either too many contractions (trying to be casual) or none at all (too formal)
       "We're" appears far more often than "we are" in fake-casual voice
Clause Patterns
   Anticipatory "it" constructions: "It is important to note that..." / "It should be emphasized that..."
   Existential "there" overuse: "There are several factors that..." / "There exists a need for..."
   Passive voice hedging: "It has been shown that..." / "It can be argued that..."
   Cleft sentences for false emphasis: "It is through X that Y occurs"
Lexical Choices
Vocabulary Selection
   Academic register inflation: Using formal vocabulary even in casual contexts ("utilize" vs. "use,"
   "commence" vs. "start")
   Buzzword density: Packing in trendy terms ("leverage," "synergy," "paradigm shift,"
   "revolutionary")
   Modifier stacking: Three or more adjectives per noun phrase ("comprehensive, multifaceted,
   innovative approach")
   Latin/Greek preference: Choosing Latinate words over Germanic ones ("demonstrate" vs.
   "show")
Collocational Patterns
   Stock phrases: "In today's rapidly evolving world..." / "plays a crucial role in..."
   Cliché combinations: "dive deep," "unpack the nuances," "navigate the complexities"
   Redundant intensifiers: "very unique," "extremely essential," "critically important"
Discourse Organization
Paragraph Structure
   Topic sentence rigidity: Every paragraph starts with an obvious thesis statement
   Mechanical transitions: Using the same transition words in predictable patterns
   Five-paragraph essay syndrome: Introduction, three body paragraphs, conclusion
   Circular conclusions: Ending by restating the introduction almost verbatim
Cohesion Devices
   Over-explicit connectives: "As previously mentioned," "As we have seen," "Building upon this"
   Demonstrative obsession: "This approach," "These factors," "That concept" (often
   unnecessarily)
   Additive conjunction chains: "Also... Moreover... Furthermore... Additionally..." in sequence
Semantic Patterns
Meaning Construction
   Semantic bleaching: Using words that sound meaningful but add little ("significant," "notable,"
   "considerable")
   Tautological statements: "Success requires successful strategies"
   Vague quantifiers: "numerous," "various," "multiple" without specifics
   Abstract concept chains: Linking abstract nouns without concrete examples
Propositional Content
   Surface-level analysis: Stating obvious points as if profound
   False dichotomies: "Not just X, but also Y" when both are obviously true
   Hedging overload: "Perhaps," "might," "could potentially," "may possibly"
Pragmatic Features
Register Inconsistency
   Formality mismatches: Academic vocabulary in personal narratives
   Genre confusion: Using essay conventions in creative writing
   Audience blindness: Same tone regardless of context
Information Structure
   Given-new violations: Presenting known information as novel insights
   Theme-rheme predictability: Always following subject-verb-object patterns
   Focus misplacement: Emphasizing minor points with major linguistic resources
Stylistic Markers
Rhetorical Devices
   Rule of three addiction: Everything comes in triplets
   False balance: "On one hand... on the other hand" for non-opposing ideas
   Rhetorical questions: Used mechanically to transition between sections
Prosodic Patterns
   Rhythmic monotony: Similar sentence lengths throughout
   Cadence predictability: Ending patterns that feel scripted
   Stress pattern uniformity: Lack of natural emphasis variation
   Paragraph length uniformity: Consistent 3-5 sentence paragraphs, never single-sentence
   emphasis or extended rambling
   Mechanical paragraph structure: Topic sentence → 2-3 supporting sentences →
   conclusion/transition
Computational Tells
Statistical Regularities
   Lexical diversity issues: Either too high (thesaurus abuse) or too low (repetition)
   N-gram predictability: Common phrase sequences appearing verbatim
   Sentence length distribution: Unnaturally consistent sentence lengths
Logical Structure
   Perfect organization: Never meandering or naturally digressive
   Exhaustive listing: Trying to cover every possible angle
   Symmetrical argumentation: Perfectly balanced pro/con sections
Verbosity Patterns
Expansion Habits
   Coverage anxiety: Including every potentially relevant point rather than focusing on what matters
   Elaboration compulsion: Never letting a simple statement stand alone ("The sky is blue" → "The
   sky exhibits a blue coloration due to atmospheric light scattering")
    Example inflation: Providing three examples when one would suffice
    Redundant clarification: "In other words," "That is to say," "To put it another way"
Linguistic Padding
    Nominalization excess: "give consideration to" instead of "consider"
    Periphrastic constructions: "is able to" instead of "can"
    Filler phrases: "It is worth noting that," "It should be mentioned that"
    Unnecessary precision: "approximately 7-10 days" when "about a week" would do
Completeness Compulsion
    Both-sides-ism: Always including "however" and counterpoints even when unnecessary
    Exhaustive listing: Trying to cover every angle rather than the most important ones
    Defensive writing: Adding qualifiers to avoid being wrong
    Academic inflation: Using formal register even in casual requests
Meta-Linguistic Observations
Paragraph Length as a Tell
The 3-5 sentence paragraph consistency reveals:
   Textbook conditioning: Mimicking "ideal" paragraph structure from writing guides
   Lack of emotional variation: No short, punchy paragraphs for emphasis or long, flowing ones for
   complex ideas
   Template thinking: Each paragraph follows topic → support → transition formula
   Missing the rhetorical power of variation: Human writers use paragraph length as a tool; AI
   treats it as a constant
The Absence of Human Messiness
The most damning tell is what's missing: the writing lacks "disfluencies" — the natural irregularities,
personality quirks, and authentic voice that make human writing feel alive. No:
    Sentence fragments for emphasis
    Inconsistent stylistic choices
    Personal linguistic tics
    Regional or cultural markers
    Emotional overflow affecting syntax
    Stream-of-consciousness digressions
The "Trying to Sound Human" Paradox
Even when attempting casual or natural writing, AI falls into new predictable patterns:
   Performative casualness: Overuse of contractions, strategic profanity
   Template switching: Moving between preset "voices" rather than having one authentic voice
   Consistent inconsistency: Being "random" in predictable ways
   Meta-awareness loops: Constantly commenting on own artificiality
   Formulaic spontaneity: Even "stream of consciousness" follows patterns

Common Em Dash Patterns
    "Success isn't just about working hard — it's about working smart"
    "This isn't merely a technological shift — it's a fundamental transformation"
    "Not because it's easy — but because it's necessary"
    "It's not just about the destination — it's about the journey"
Conversational Template Patterns
    Fake casual openers: "You know what?", "Here's the thing:", "The real [x]?", "But here's the catch
    —"
    Ellipsis abuse: Using "..." to simulate thoughtful pauses or trailing thoughts
    Calculated informality: Strategic f-bombs and "gonna," "wanna" placement
    Meta-commentary interruptions: "(Did you notice I just...?)" "(See what I did there?)"
    Performance markers: "Watch this:", "Check it out:", "Get this:"
Template Switching
    Formal academic template: Moreover, Furthermore, It is important to note
    Casual conversational template: You know what?, Here's the thing, trailing off...
    Self-aware meta template: Catching myself mid-sentence, commenting on own writing
    Rebellious/edgy template: Calculated profanity, sentence fragments for effect
    Each template has predictable patterns that still reveal AI origin
Rule of Three Examples
    "This approach is efficient, effective, and elegant"
    "Students become more engaged, motivated, and successful"
    "The results were surprising, significant, and transformative"
    "We must act with courage, conviction, and compassion"
Patterns That Persist After "Humanizing" Attempts
These patterns often survive even when AI tries to edit out other tells:
Structural Patterns
    Em dashes for definition: "Clear the grunt work – Generate boilerplate"
    Colons in titles: Still appears even in "casual" rewrites
    List items starting with verbs: "Generate...", "Flag...", "Propose..."
    Binary constructions: "juniors climb... seniors stay..."
Sneaky Formulations
    "Of course" as transition: Sounds natural but overused by AI
    The payoff framing: "For teams, the payoff is..."
    Confident prediction endings: "will ship better software, faster"
    Meta-category descriptions: "What the current crop can do"
    Feature listing with em dashes: Each bullet point follows "Action – Explanation" format
    Temporal bridge phrases: "Researchers are already..." "AI will not... yet it is already..."
    Industry insider voice: "Nobody misses the slog" (trying to sound experienced)
Replacement Templates
When trying to avoid one tell, AI often just swaps in another:
  "Moreover" → "Picture this:"
  "It is important to note" → "Nobody misses"
  "In conclusion" → "Those who... will..."
  Academic voice → Fake startup blog voice
Metaphor and Analogy Patterns
    Crystalline/technical metaphors: "crystal balls," "wiring models into shells"
    Forced workplace analogies: Comparing everything to tools, crafts, or journeys
    Mixed metaphor chains: "climb the learning curve," "stay focused," "feels lighter" (too many in
    succession)
    Ending on fortune cookie wisdom: "Those who keep their fundamentals sharp... will ship better
    software, faster"
Information Architecture Tells
    The setup-list-conclusion format: Opening paragraph → bulleted list → philosophical ending
    Hedging followed by certainty: "Of course, these systems still make errors" → "AI will not
    replace... yet"
    Contrived specificity: "build a REST endpoint for products by tag" (oddly specific example)
    Time consciousness: "while your coffee cools" (trying too hard to be relatable)
